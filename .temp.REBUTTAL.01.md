## 3. Tighten the PostgreSQL 17 HA and logical-exchange contract

Retain **two three-member Patroni groups**, each spanning the three physical failure domains, with three shared DCS voters and separate namespaces and credentials.

The HertzBeat domain owns `hertzbeat`, initially preferring trixie. The vector domain owns `lme_vectors` and `litellm`, initially preferring graph. Opposite-domain logical copies remain application-read-only and are never substituted for physical standbys.

### 3.1 Define what “application-table copies” permits

Use explicit, reviewed publication inventories per database. Review table and column contents for stored service passwords, provider credentials, tokens, sensitive configuration, and restricted research material before authorizing exchange.

A publication is a replication selection mechanism, **not a complete authorization boundary**. PostgreSQL’s logical-replication security model requires separate attention to replication roles, database access, and publisher privileges. ([PostgreSQL][5])

Where a native table cannot be copied safely, use a separately identified, sanitized physical projection table. Do not call that a complete native-table replica, and do not publish a view as though native logical replication supports it. PostgreSQL 17 supports table replication, not views or materialized views. ([PostgreSQL][6])

Keep imported tables out of reciprocal publications. Use `origin=none` as additional protection, not as a substitute for disjoint ownership and initial-copy checks; PostgreSQL notes that initial copying does not establish the original provenance of rows already imported upstream. ([PostgreSQL][7])

Install compatible pgvector binaries and extensions wherever vector-typed relations may exist, including importing domains. Test retrieval correctness against an exact vector-search baseline and preserve model, dimension, normalization, and distance-function metadata.

### 3.2 Make logical-slot readiness part of promotion eligibility

PostgreSQL 17 native failover-slot configuration requires more than `failover=true`: standby slot synchronization, a physical replication slot, hot-standby feedback, and a valid connection database are relevant prerequisites. Slot synchronization is asynchronous, so candidate readiness must be checked before promotion. ([PostgreSQL][8])

Bind promotion eligibility to both physical durability and logical continuity. Verify required persistent, synchronized, noninvalidated slots, including applicable table-synchronization slots, on candidates. After promotion, verify usable slot positions and resumed subscriptions rather than applying standby-only status checks to the new primary. ([PostgreSQL][9])

**Important:** `synchronized_standby_slots` waits for every listed physical slot, not “any one standby.” A stale or unavailable listed slot can stall logical delivery while ordinary synchronous database writes remain possible. Its membership must be reconciled with promotion eligibility and current roles; never clear it silently to pass a recovery test. ([PostgreSQL][10])

Test publisher failover and subscriber failover independently. Subscriber tests must verify imported data, replication-origin progress, apply durability, and continued consumption. Publisher failover settings alone do not demonstrate subscriber recovery.

### 3.3 Keep fencing compatible with two domains per guest

Require one safe synchronous standby and strict durability for the HA acceptance profile. Patroni documents the availability tradeoff: strict synchronous operation can block writes when the required safe replication state is unavailable. ([Patroni][11])

Fencing needs an explicit owner and bounded target. Do not give two independent domain owners uncontrolled access to the same watchdog or guest-reset mechanism. A guest-wide reset affects both database members and all colocated applications; that blast radius belongs in the tests.

Use a reviewed, narrowly privileged mechanism that can establish that the former writer cannot continue accepting writes. Endpoint removal alone is not sufficient evidence. Patroni’s watchdog documentation specifically addresses cases where ordinary process-level demotion may fail. ([Patroni][12])

No fencing operation may reset a physical host running excluded workloads.

### 3.4 Migrations, reset, and restore

Replace `docs_chunks` drop/recreate with a managed reset that preserves the published relation. Choose tested `DELETE` or `TRUNCATE` semantics after checking foreign keys and publication behavior; neither is a universal substitution.

Coordinate subscriber-compatible DDL, publisher changes, and application rollout. Logical replication does not copy DDL or advance sequences, and replicated truncation has foreign-key restrictions. ([PostgreSQL][6])

Keep migration credentials separate from runtime identities. Prevent concurrent application startup from racing schema migrations across the HA members.

Validate `shared-ha` on fresh owned resources as the alternative: one three-member group, separate application databases, no reciprocal logical subscriptions. Switching database profiles on retained data is a migration, not a harmless flag change.

Backups, archived WAL, isolated restore, and recovery-key availability remain initial acceptance requirements.

## 7. Define durability and recovery guarantees at their actual boundaries

Keep the 4 GiB log spool, 2 GiB free-disk reserve, 24-hour post-verification retention, 256 MiB metric queues per selected destination, and independent delivery obligations. State their scope per spool/node/destination and budget aggregate consumption.

Never automatically purge unverified records. Profile changes govern new admissions; existing obligations remain until verified or explicitly cancelled through an audited operator action.

**Distinguish three acknowledgment boundaries:**

| Acknowledgment                | Required meaning                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PostgreSQL commit success     | The selected synchronous database durability contract was satisfied.                |
| SQLite admission success      | The record and obligations were durably committed to that spool’s storage.          |
| Destination transport success | The sink accepted the request; searchable-content verification remains outstanding. |

A local SQLite acknowledgment does not prove survival of permanent loss of that storage. Preserve the bridge design, but state whether the failure test is a restart with retained disks or permanent storage loss. Claiming protection against the latter requires an additional independently durable copy before acknowledgment.

### Make the numerical targets measurable

**Zero loss of acknowledged writes:** scope this to positively acknowledged database transactions under the tested failure model, with an independent witness recording operation IDs outside the failed guest. Treat ambiguous client outcomes separately and resolve them through idempotent readback.

**Application reconnection within 90 seconds:** measure from failure injection to a successful authenticated business operation by a surviving client. A readiness socket or database ping is not that operation.

Also distinguish database HA from application HA. Under this placement, graph hosts LME, trixie hosts HertzBeat/Greptime, and silmaril hosts Malcolm/AI. Their database replicas do not keep a singleton application running after its guest disappears. Do not report whole-platform 90-second recovery unless application redundancy is separately implemented and budgeted.

**Retained-event recovery within five minutes:** identify a backlog high-water mark at destination restoration and verify every retained obligation through that mark while new traffic continues.

The basic capacity constraint is:

$$
T_{\text{drain}}\ \geq\ \frac{B}{\mu-\lambda},\qquad \mu>\lambda
$$

Here \(B\) is retained backlog, \(\lambda\) is continuing arrival rate, and \(\mu\) is effective verified-delivery throughput. Indexing delay, retry scheduling, and readback add further latency. Test a declared backlog and load rather than presenting five minutes as independent of volume.
